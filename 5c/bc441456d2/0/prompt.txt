I will say this through gritted teeth: the write-up is better than most production incident postmortems I have read, the classifier is checked in both directions against real logs, and the is<Thing>=true versus bare-output-name detail is genuinely subtle and correctly handled. The prose changes to care-review are fine. The lock file matches the frontmatter. No complaints there.

The new workflow is where the bugs live, and they are all in the supersede guard:

The freshness check runs before a 10-20 minute sleep. It answers the question at the one moment it cannot possibly matter. If a push lands during the wait - and that wait is long enough that it will - you re-run against a stale SHA anyway. This defeats the stated purpose of the step.
per_page=1 plus workflow_runs[0] is a bet on undocumented ordering, and since a re-run does not mint a new run id, after the first retry the guard compares $RUN_ID against itself and passes unconditionally.
Concurrency keyed on workflow_run.id collapses all attempts into one slot with cancel-in-progress: false, so attempts serialize instead of running independently.
None of these break the happy path, which is why they will be a nuisance to debug at 3am rather than obvious now. Fix 1 and 2 together - move the check after the sleep and compare timestamps, not array position - and this is fine.
